# Ethics and boundaries

*Beyond the Questions* is designed to sit inside the ICF Code of Ethics and the ICF Artificial Intelligence Coaching Framework and Standards (2024). It does not replace them. Read both:
- ICF Code of Ethics: https://coachingfederation.org/ethics/code-of-ethics
- ICF AI Coaching Framework and Standards: https://coachingfederation.org/resource/icf-artificial-intelligence-ai-coaching-framework-and-standards

## 1. Client confidentiality comes first
- Use only **anonymized** material: no names, companies, places, dates, health details or anything else that could identify the client or the sponsor.
- Prefer **short excerpts** or **reconstructed episodes** to full transcripts.
- Make sure you have the **right to use** the material for your own development. Where recordings or transcripts are involved, this normally means the client's informed consent, in line with your coaching agreement and the data-protection law that applies to you (e.g. GDPR in the EU).
- Under the ICF Code of Ethics, the coach remains responsible for confidentiality **including through any technology they use**. Before using any AI assistant, check where your data is stored, who can access it, and whether it is used to train models. Use settings or plans that exclude training on your content where available.

## 2. Not supervision, not mentor coaching
This is a structured form of **self-reflection**. It complements, but does not replace:
- **coaching supervision** with a qualified supervisor, and
- **mentor coaching** with an ICF-qualified mentor coach.
The AI has no relationship with you over time, no embodied presence, no accountability, and can be wrong.

## 3. Not assessment or certification
The skill **never scores** sessions and **never states** whether an ICF competency or PCC marker was demonstrated. Its output is not evidence of competence for any credential.

## 4. Fallibility
The AI's readings are **hypotheses** generated from text. It can misread tone, culture, context and silence. You are the authority on your own experience: discard what doesn't fit.

## 5. Ethical dilemmas and wellbeing
If the reflection surfaces an ethical dilemma, or significant personal distress, take it to a supervisor, a peer or the ICF Ethics resources. The skill is instructed to name such issues neutrally and point you there, not to resolve them.
