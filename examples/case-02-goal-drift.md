# Case 02: The goal that quietly changed

*Fictional case written for illustration. Any resemblance to real people is coincidental.*

**Focus:** losing the session agreement while following interesting content. **Competencies touched:** CC3 Establishes and Maintains Agreements, CC5 Maintains Presence, CC2 Embodies a Coaching Mindset.
**Distinctions offered:** declarations and shared standards; transparency and breakdown.

## Remembered episode described by the coach

> "The client wanted to prepare a difficult conversation with her board about a budget cut. We agreed she'd leave with an opening line and two key messages. About 20 minutes in, she mentioned that her father had always told her 'never show weakness'. It was fascinating, and we spent the rest of the session on that. At the end she said it was 'really interesting', but she had no opening line. I felt I'd done deep work, but I'm not sure."

## Phase 1: Moments

**AI:** Three moments I'd point to, without evaluating them:
1. The agreement at the start: an opening line plus two key messages. (CC3)
2. "My father always said: never show weakness" → the session follows this thread. (CC3, CC5, CC6)
3. The close: "really interesting", no opening line. (CC3, CC8)

Where would you like to start?
**Coach:** The second one.

## Phase 2: Reflective dialogue (abridged)

**AI:** When she mentioned her father's words, what happened in you?
**Coach:** I got excited. It felt like the "real" issue behind the presentation.

**AI:** Who decided that the session would now be about that?
**Coach:** …Me, I think. I didn't ask her.

**AI:** What were you hoping would happen by following that thread?
**Coach:** A breakthrough. A deep session. Honestly, the kind of session I'm proud of.

**AI:** One possible lens, offered as a hypothesis. The session goal was a shared *declaration*: an agreement about what you'd work on and how you'd both recognize success. When the father's sentence appeared, the agreement went "transparent", meaning it stopped being visible, and a new, unspoken goal took its place: depth. That goal may have been more yours than hers. How does that sit with you?
**Coach:** Uncomfortable, but true. "Deep" was my standard of success, not hers.

**AI:** What other choice was available at that point?
**Coach:** To name it: "This seems important. Do you want to stay with it, or keep to the board conversation?" Or to link the two: how does "never show weakness" show up in the opening line?

**AI:** What does this say about the coach you were being?
**Coach:** A coach who needs sessions to feel profound. That's about me.

## Phase 3: Integration

**AI:** What do you see now that you didn't see before?
**Coach:** That I can drop the agreement without noticing when something feels "deep".
**AI:** What will you try next time?
**Coach:** When the topic shifts, I'll check the agreement out loud and let the client choose. I'll notice I'm drifting when I feel that excitement.
**AI:** Anything for supervision?
**Coach:** My need for "deep" sessions, and where it comes from.

## Reflection log (coach's words)
- **Level:** being
- **Distinction:** agreement as a shared declaration; my standards vs. the client's
- **Experiment:** name every topic shift and let the client choose
- **To supervision:** my need for profound sessions
