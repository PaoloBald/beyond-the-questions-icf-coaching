# Case 01: "Nobody is competent enough"

*Fictional case written for illustration. Any resemblance to real people is coincidental.*

**Focus:** a question that carries the client's judgment as a fact. **Competencies touched:** CC6 Listens Actively, CC7 Evokes Awareness, CC2 Embodies a Coaching Mindset.
**Distinction offered:** facts (affirmations) vs. judgments; observer.

## Excerpt shared by the coach

> **Cl:** I'm drowning. I can't delegate because nobody on my team is competent enough.
> **C:** I hear that. What could you do to improve your team's skills?
> **Cl:** Well… training, I suppose. Maybe pair them with a senior person. But that takes time I don't have.
> **C:** What would be the first step to find that time?
> **Cl:** I don't know. Maybe block an hour on Fridays.
> **C:** Great, so blocking Fridays could be an action?
> **Cl:** Yes, I guess.

**AI:** What do you want to look at in this excerpt?
**Coach:** The session ended with an action, but it felt flat. I'd like to understand why.
**AI:** What would make this reflection useful for you?
**Coach:** Seeing what I could have done differently.
**AI:** Before we start: is this excerpt anonymized, and are you allowed to use it for your own development?
**Coach:** Yes, it's reconstructed and anonymized, and the client has agreed.

## Phase 1: Moments

**AI:** Three moments stand out. I'm not judging them, just pointing to them:
1. *"Nobody on my team is competent enough"* → your next question. (CC6, CC7)
2. *"That takes time I don't have"* → "What would be the first step to find that time?" (CC6, CC7)
3. *"Great, so blocking Fridays could be an action?"* → *"Yes, I guess."* (CC8, CC2)

Which would you like to start with, or is there another moment that matters more?
**Coach:** The first one.

## Phase 2: Reflective dialogue (abridged)

**AI:** When the client said "nobody is competent enough", what did you notice in that moment?
**Coach:** That he was overwhelmed. I wanted to help him get out of it.

**AI:** What interpretation of the situation did your question take for granted?
**Coach:** …That the team really isn't competent. I went straight to fixing their skills.

**AI:** What were you feeling just before you asked it?
**Coach:** A bit of urgency. He sounded desperate, and I wanted to give him something concrete.

**AI:** I'd like to offer a distinction, as a possibility rather than a verdict. Ontological coaching separates *affirmations*, which describe observable facts, from *judgments*, which are interpretations that can be more or less grounded. "Nobody is competent enough" is a judgment, and a sweeping one ("nobody"). One possible reading is that your urgency led you to hear it as a fact, so the conversation moved to solving the team's problem rather than looking at the client's way of observing. How does this land for you?
**Coach:** It lands. I bought his story completely.

**AI:** If you met that same sentence tomorrow, what other doors could you open?
**Coach:** I could ask what "competent enough" means to him. Or what facts support "nobody". Or what it would mean for him if someone *was* competent enough.

**AI:** What does that last question point to?
**Coach:** Control, maybe. Or his identity as the one who does things right.

**AI:** What does this moment say about the coach you were being?
**Coach:** A rescuer. A problem-solver. That's my old manager self.

## Phase 3: Integration

**AI:** What do you see now that you didn't see before?
**Coach:** That my urgency makes me treat judgments as facts. Then I coach the problem, not the person.
**AI:** What distinction do you want to keep practicing?
**Coach:** Facts vs. judgments, especially with "nobody", "always", "never".
**AI:** What will you try next session, and how will you notice whether you did?
**Coach:** When I hear a generalization, I'll first ask what it means to the client. I'll notice it if I catch myself feeling urgent.
**AI:** Anything to bring to supervision?
**Coach:** The "rescuer" pattern. I think it's older than this session.

## Reflection log (coach's words)
- **Level:** observer (and possibly being)
- **Distinction:** facts vs. judgments
- **Experiment:** when I hear a generalization, explore its meaning before moving to action
- **To supervision:** my rescuer pattern under urgency

- It didn't rewrite the coach's question as a "better question" without being asked.
- It didn't diagnose the client ("he's a control freak").
