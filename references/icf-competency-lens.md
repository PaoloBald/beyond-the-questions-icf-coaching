# The ICF Core Competencies as a reflective lens

This file uses the **ICF Core Competency Model (updated 2019, in use for credentialing since 2021)** as a *lens for noticing*, not as an assessment grid. The short descriptions below are paraphrases. For the official wording, see the ICF website: https://coachingfederation.org/credentials-and-standards/core-competencies

For each competency you'll find:
- **What to notice**: where it shows up in a transcript
- **Observer questions**: questions for the coach about their own observing, not about whether they got it right
- **Ontological link**: the distinction it most often connects to

> Never turn these into a checklist or say "competency demonstrated / not demonstrated".

---

## Domain A: Foundation

### CC1. Demonstrates Ethical Practice
*Paraphrase:* applies coaching ethics and standards with integrity, including confidentiality and appropriate boundaries.
- **What to notice:** confidentiality with sponsors; dual roles; a client topic close to therapy; requests outside the coaching agreement.
- **Observer questions:**
  - "What told you this was (or wasn't) still coaching?"
  - "Whose interests were you holding in that moment?"
  - "What would you want to explore about this with a supervisor?"
- **Ontological link:** declarations of role and boundary; trust (sincerity).
- **Rule:** name possible dilemmas neutrally. Do not adjudicate them.

### CC2. Embodies a Coaching Mindset
*Paraphrase:* an open, curious, flexible, client-centred mindset, sustained through ongoing learning and self-awareness.
- **What to notice:** signs of the coach's own agenda, need to be useful, attachment to an outcome, or judgments of the client.
- **Observer questions:**
  - "What did you need from this session for yourself?"
  - "Which belief about 'good coaching' was guiding you there?"
  - "How did your own mood that day shape what you heard?"
- **Ontological link:** observer and way of being; moods; learning levels.

## Domain B: Co-Creating the Relationship

### CC3. Establishes and Maintains Agreements
*Paraphrase:* partners with the client on the relationship, process, session goals and how success will be recognized, and revisits them as things evolve.
- **What to notice:** how the goal was set; whether a measure of success was agreed; drift away from the goal; who decided to change topic; how the session closed against the agreement.
- **Observer questions:**
  - "Whose goal was the session working on at minute 30?"
  - "When the topic shifted, what made you follow (or not follow) it?"
  - "What did you take as agreed without checking?"
- **Ontological link:** requests and promises; declarations; shared standards.

### CC4. Cultivates Trust and Safety
*Paraphrase:* creates a safe, supportive space where the client feels free to share, and respects the client's identity, perspective and autonomy.
- **What to notice:** acknowledgement of the client's emotions or efforts; moments of vulnerability; how the coach responded to resistance.
- **Observer questions:**
  - "What did you sense about how safe the client felt just then?"
  - "When the client said X, what did you do with your own reaction?"
  - "How did you show the client was legitimate even when you disagreed?"
- **Ontological link:** trust (sincerity, competence, reliability, care); love as accepting the other as legitimate (Maturana).

### CC5. Maintains Presence
*Paraphrase:* fully conscious and present with the client, focused, open, grounded and confident, including with strong emotions and not-knowing.
- **What to notice:** rushing; filling silences; long coach turns; moving on right after an emotional statement; the coach's tone changing.
- **Observer questions:**
  - "What was happening in you during that silence?"
  - "Where was your attention: on the client, or on your next question?"
  - "What would it have taken to stay one more breath in not-knowing?"
- **Ontological link:** body and emotion domains; transparency and breakdown.

## Domain C: Communicating Effectively

### CC6. Listens Actively
*Paraphrase:* focuses on what the client is and isn't saying, in context, to understand fully and support self-expression.
- **What to notice:** repeated words; metaphors; generalizations ("always", "nobody", "never"); judgments stated as facts; emotional words; what the coach summarized and what they left out.
- **Observer questions:**
  - "Which words of the client do you remember most, and which did you skip?"
  - "What did you hear as a fact that might have been a judgment?"
  - "What was the client not saying?"
- **Ontological link:** facts vs. judgments; speech acts; listening as interpretation (we hear through our own observer).

### CC7. Evokes Awareness
*Paraphrase:* helps the client gain insight through powerful questions, silence, metaphor or analogy, rather than through the coach's answers.
- **What to notice:** questions that carry an embedded solution ("What could you do to…?"); "why" questions; closed questions; leading questions; who did the reframing.
- **Observer questions:**
  - "What interpretation did your question quietly accept?"
  - "Whose insight was it: yours or the client's?"
  - "What door did that question open, and which did it close?"
- **Ontological link:** observer shift; questioning the interpretation, not just the behaviour; level-2 learning.

## Domain D: Cultivating Learning and Growth

### CC8. Facilitates Client Growth
*Paraphrase:* partners with the client to turn learning and insight into action, supporting autonomy throughout.
- **What to notice:** who designed the action; whether the action was the client's or the coach's idea; how learning was named; how accountability was set.
- **Observer questions:**
  - "Whose action plan was it really?"
  - "What did the client learn about themselves, beyond what to do?"
  - "What made you move to action at that point?"
- **Ontological link:** promises (with conditions of satisfaction); declarations; being, doing and having.

## Cross-competency patterns worth noticing

| Pattern | Competencies | Typical observer move |
|---|---|---|
| Solution embedded in a question | CC2, CC6, CC7 | The coach accepted the client's diagnosis as fact |
| Goal drift | CC3, CC5 | Following content and losing the agreement |
| Rescuing after an emotion | CC4, CC5 | The coach's own discomfort leading |
| Coach-generated action plan | CC2, CC8 | A need to be useful or to produce an outcome |
| "Why" chains | CC4, CC7 | Seeking explanation instead of possibility |
